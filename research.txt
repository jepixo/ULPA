The Accommodation Service at the University of Limerick has some advice to aid students searching off campus self-catering accommodation. This is advisory information and a quick reference to some of the main issues involved in the renting of private accommodation. It is not in any way or should not be taken as a substitute for legal advice regarding tenancies/tenants’ rights or obligations. The University accepts no liability whatsoever by virtue of placing any accommodation on its accommodation listing.

Living off campus for the first time can sometimes feel daunting so we have put together this guide to give you some helpful hints and tips. 

Choosing your area:

Areas within walking distance to the University include Castletroy, Monaleen, Annacotty, Golf Links Road, Rhebogue.

Bus links to the University http://www.ul.ie/ee/uploads/files/3-%20Bus%20Route%20Map%20.pdf

What to check houses for when viewing:

    Dampness; is there any on the walls or ceilings
    Is there sufficient ventilation?
    How is the house heated?
    What appliances are supplied and do they work?  – cooker,  fridge, freezer, electric showers, heating system
    How are the bills paid? Whose name will the bill be in? Is it metered or bill pay?
    What are the refuse arrangements?
    What furniture is in the house and what condition is it in?
    Are there smoke alarms and an escape route?
    How many keys are there and who will have access? Check that doors have locks and are secure. Check that windows open and have secure locks.

DEPOSIT

Most Landlords will request a deposit of approximately 1 months’ rent. Always request a receipt.

Generally the Landlord will hold the deposit as security against damages, unpaid rent or a tenant breaching the tenancy agreement.

You may forfeit some or all of your deposit if you:

    1. Do not give proper notice or leave before the tenancy agreement expires
    2. Damage the property above normal wear and tear (ask for receipts for anything deducted as damages)
    3. Leave unpaid bills or rent (again ask for receipts)

Students should note that their deposit does not cover rent when they have given notice to the landlord or have been served notice by the landlord to leave the property

RENT

Key questions to ask:

    How much is the rent?
    What does the rent include
    How many weeks rent do you need to pay in advance?
    What date is rent due and is it weekly or monthly?
    How much notice should you give, if less than the minimum legal notice of 28 – 35 days in writing?
    Ask for a rent book
    Get receipts of all payments

KEY RECOMMENDATIONS

Always request a copy of the LETTING AGREEMENT before you sign a lease / contract.

    Request a rent book – This will set out how much rent is payable and specify contact details for your landlord.
    Ensure the landlord gives a receipt for each payment and signs the rent book
    Check if the landlord is registered with PRTB.
    Establish the landlord’s right of access to the property.
    Take pictures of all rooms in the houses and the exterior before you move inand when you move out. If possible have dates on the pictures. This is vital evidence for dispute resolutions
    Keep all receipts, letters and bills related to the house.
    Request an inventory – a list of all items in the house, plus any structural damage / wear and tear in the house. Ensure landlord and tenants sign it.

Tenants’ Rights and Obligations

A full list of tenants and landlords rights and responsibilities are outlined in the Residential Tenancies Act 2004 available at www.irishstatutebook.ie . A quick guide is also available on www.prtb.ie .

This is not applicable if the house is owner occupied.

Insurance

Landlords should ensure that they are covered by standard public liability insurance and fire and theft insurance before renting a property. Students are advised to insure their personal belongings, as it is unlikely that the property owner’s policy will cover personal effects.

Damage

Students are required to make good any damage they or their friends may do to the property of effects of the property owner, provided that such damage is not due to normal wear and tear.

Student Problems

Unsuitable accommodation, financial difficulty, loneliness, nervous strain, study difficulty are the sort of problems students may experience. If you do experience accommodation difficulties, please call to the Accommodation Office, Student Centre for guidance. If you experience any of the other problems listed, a counselling service is available to you free of charge. Call to Room CM073 for all queries regarding counselling.

Legal Agreements

Students are advised to exercise extreme caution before entering into tenancies for long periods.

Any agreement made with a landlord is binding in law, whether it is made verbally or in writing. In the interest of both parties it is strongly recommended that the agreement is in writing.

If you are unsure of the exact terms of the agreement do not sign it until you have sought advice. The agreement is designed to clarify the rights and duties of both householder and student tenant(s) and so help avoid unnecessary disputes.

Tenancy Termination

The landlord may terminate without specifying grounds the first 6 months, after which the landlord can terminate under specific grounds (See Residential Tenancies Act 2004).

Shorter notice periods apply where terminate is for non-compliance with tenancy obligations (7 days for anti-social behaviour, 28 days for other breaches) and the parties may agree a shorter notice period at the time of termination.

Dispute Resolution

The Private Residential Tenancy Board (PRTB) has been set up to resolve disputes between landlords and tenants. Anyone wishing to refer a dispute to the PRTB should contact them directly in the first instance and discuss the matter.

Respect your Neighbours

When you move into your chosen home you should respect your neighbours and the community you live in. There are many families that may be living near you and we ask students to act responsibly.

## Step 1 — You do NOT “apply to a village”

You apply through the **UL Accommodation Portal** and rank preferences.

You do not apply separately to:

* Kilmurry
* Plassey
* Dromroe
* Cappavilla

You submit one application and list choices in order.

## Step 2 — Allocation Depends on Category + Availability

UL allocates rooms based on:

1. Student category

   * Undergraduate
   * Postgraduate
   * International
2. Contract length (38, 41, 51 weeks)
3. Availability at time of allocation
4. Application timing

Even if a village says “suitable for postgraduates,” that only means:

* Postgraduates can be placed there
* It is not reserved for postgraduates

Undergraduate demand is much higher, so some villages are effectively undergrad-heavy.

---

## Step 3 — How a Postgrad Gets Into Kilmurry / Plassey / Dromroe

For a postgraduate to get into one of these:

1. Apply early when accommodation opens.
2. Select those villages in your preference ranking.
3. Choose the contract length available for your category.
4. Accept quickly if offered.

If postgraduate-designated villages (like Cappavilla or Thomond) fill up, UL may allocate postgrads into:

* Dromroe
* Plassey
* Kilmurry

But this depends entirely on supply that year.

For 51-week contracts:
UL prioritizes certain villages for postgraduate allocations.

If you want:

* Highest probability as a postgrad → Cappavilla / Thomond
* Possible but not guaranteed → Dromroe / Plassey

When portal opens:

1. Apply within first days.
2. Rank villages exactly how you want them.
3. Select 51-week if required.
4. Email accommodation office stating:

   * You are postgraduate
   * You prefer X village
   * You are flexible on room type

This increases chances if there is flexibility in allocation.

## Bottom Line

You do not “secure” Kilmurry as a postgrad directly.

You:

* Apply through portal
* Rank preferences
* Get allocated based on availability and category

Here’s a clear explanation of **whether on-campus room prices differ for postgraduate vs undergraduate at University of Limerick**, and **how the accommodation application process works**:

---

# **1. Do on-campus villages charge different prices for postgrads vs undergrads?**

**Official published fees** from UL show that:

* UL publishes **postgraduate accommodation rates that are specific to postgraduate contracts** (51 weeks, ensuite private bedroom, utilities included). ([University of Limerick][1])
* Those postgraduate rates are set based on the contract type (e.g., 51-week). ([University of Limerick][1])

However:

### **Villages like Plassey, Kilmurry, Dromroe, etc. don’t have separate “postgraduate pricing” published publicly**

* They are listed as “suitable for postgrads” on general pages, meaning **postgraduates *can be allocated there***. ([University of Limerick][2])
* But UL doesn’t break out *different fees for the same room type* just because you’re postgrad vs undergrad *on the public fees page*.

**So in practice:**

* If a postgraduate gets placed in a room type at one of those villages, their fee is usually the *standard published rate for that room type and contract length*, not a discounted postgraduate amount.
* Postgraduate-specific fee listings shown on the postgraduate page are usually for *designated postgraduate allocations or contracts* (e.g., Quigley Village, Cappavilla for 51 weeks). ([University of Limerick][3])

That means:

* **It’s possible that the same ensuite room type has the same price for undergrad or postgrad** if the contract and room category are identical.
* “Postgraduate” pricing often appears only in the context of specific postgraduate-focused offers (e.g., full academic or 51-week blocks).

---

# **2. UL Accommodation Application Procedure (step by step)**

Everything is done through the **UL Accommodation Portal**. ([University of Limerick][4])

## **Step A — Create an accommodation profile**

1. Go to the UL accommodation portal:
   [https://campuslifeweb.ul.ie/apply/Pages/Registration/Lander.aspx](https://campuslifeweb.ul.ie/apply/Pages/Registration/Lander.aspx) ([Campus Life Web][5])
2. Register with your UL student ID details (or apply once you have received your university offer).

---

## **Step B — Fill the application**

1. Enter personal details, student status (undergrad/postgrad).
2. Choose your **academic year (e.g., 2026/27)**.
3. Select **contract type** you want (e.g., 51 weeks).
4. **Rank villages in order of preference** — you might list Cappavilla first, Dromroe second, etc.

UL may allow a limited number of choices.

---

## **Step C — Submit and wait for allocation**

* UL does *not guarantee* you get your first preference.
* Allocations are released based on **category (postgrad), date of application, and supply**.
* If you don’t get an offer in the first round, you may be placed on a waitlist or re-offered later. ([University of Limerick][4])

---

## **Step D — Accept and pay**

If you receive an offer:

1. Accept the offer in the portal.
2. Pay the **deposit (€500)** and first instalment before the deadline. ([University of Limerick][1])
3. Your contract is confirmed.

---

# **3. Why Kilmurry/Plassey/Dromroe look cheaper but don’t show in postgrad fee page**

* Those villages simply **are not highlighted under the “postgraduate 51-week” block in the official fees page** because UL only lists the ones they have guaranteed blocks for postgrads. ([University of Limerick][1])
* It doesn’t mean those villages *won’t accept postgraduates* — it’s just that UL doesn’t publish a dedicated “postgraduate price list” for every village. Only some are listed on the postgrad fees page.

So **the cost for the same room type doesn’t automatically change just because you are postgrad** — it’s usually the same published tariff.

---

# **4. Where you *do* see postgraduate pricing listed**

UL’s postgraduate accommodation page shows **all-inclusive fees for postgraduate contracts**, typically including:

* Ensuite bedroom
* Utilities
* Wifi
  This is sometimes tied to specific postgraduate models like Quigley Residence or postgraduate allocations in Cappavilla/Thomond. ([University of Limerick][3])

---

# **Quick Summary**

| Question                                                                  | Answer                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Do on-campus rooms cost more or less just because of postgraduate status? | **Usually not**; fees depend on contract type and room category, not student type per se. The same room at the same contract length normally costs the same. |
| Why do some villages show lower fees?                                     | Because specific postgraduate blocks or published rates are shown and UL only lists some villages under postgraduate pricing.                                |
| How to apply?                                                             | Register on the accommodation portal → Rank preferences → Submit → Accept offer → Pay deposit/first instalment.                                              |
| Are postgraduates guaranteed places in any village?                       | No; preference, supply and timing determine allocation. Some villages are more often allocated postgraduates.                                                |
